    Overall aim

    Rain falls

    on a small basin, 100 to 2,000 km²

    →

    The river rises

    within a day or two of the rain

    →

    A warning a day ahead

    issued every evening for the next one to three days

    The question is not whether an LSTM can fit a river. It is what a warning needs: the model, or the information it is given?

    Background and the gap

    Rain that has already fallen is hidden from the forecast

    • 1

      Floods in small rain-fed basins build within a day or two of the rain, so a warning helps only if it comes a day ahead and sees the rain that has already fallen.

    • 2

      One LSTM trained on many basins is the published state of the art on the CAMELS data (Kratzert et al. 2019; Nearing et al. 2024).

    • 3

      The inputs are earth-observation products: gridded rain analyses, satellite rain, radar-and-gauge rain, elevation, land cover, and an archived weather forecast.

    Research questions

    Three questions, each with its yardstick fixed in advance

    RQ1 · objectives 2, 3

    Does the all-basin LSTM beat the best simple model on years never used for any choice?

    A gain is clear only if every training of the LSTM is above every training of the simple model and the LSTM is higher in most basins.

    RQ2 · objectives 1, 3

    Which information closes the gap: rain up to the issue time, a weather forecast, or more rivers?

    Hypothesis: rain to midnight and a forecast raise the onsets warned clearly above their controls; more rivers raise the NSE on rivers never seen.

    RQ3 · objectives 3, 4

    Can the warnings be trusted by criteria fixed in advance?

    GO needs at least 70% of onsets warned and at most 30% of warnings false. CAUTION needs a critical success index of at least 0.40. Otherwise: NOT YET.

    Objective 1Acquire, preprocess and validate the public datasets: 12 basins by rule, one basin-by-day table, the clock of every rain product measured.
    Objective 2Develop and test the workflow: a shared engine (split, scoring, trainer) with fairness checks, one LSTM for all basins, three simple baselines.
    Objective 3Quantify performance against reference data: NSE, KGE, onsets warned, false warnings, verdicts, for a ladder of information.
    Objective 4Translate into maps, datasets and recommendations: basin maps and GIS layers, the curated database, the key figure, what a service would need.

    Methodology and workflow

    One pipeline from eleven products to a scored forecast

    Step 1

    Acquire

    12 basins fixed by rule before any model runs; grids averaged to basin means; hourly rain summed over the local day; the clock of every rain product measured.

    Step 2

    Features

    Six Daymet weather columns, the river gauge, ten basin properties and each experiment's extra rain; lagged features for the simple models, checked not to read past the issue day.

    Step 3

    Models

    One LSTM for all 12 basins against three simple baselines: tomorrow = today, ridge regression, random forest. Three seeds each; early stopping on validation.

    Step 4

    Split

    Temporal, by target day: training 1980–2000, validation 2000–2005, test 2005–2014. Leave-one-basin-out as the spatial check.

    Step 5

    Scoring

    NSE, KGE, onsets warned, false warnings, CSI; GO / CAUTION / NOT YET fixed in advance; the headline numbers recomputed independently.

    The fair test

    Every choice on the validation years; the test years scored once

    Training1980–2000 · 20 years
    Validation2000–05
    Test2005–2014 · 9 years
    1980-10-012000-10-012005-10-012014-09-30
    • Scalers, flood levels and weights are fitted on the training years only; settings and the warning level are chosen on the validation years.
    • A sample belongs to the part that holds its target day, so no forecast reads past its issue time.
    • A leak test on made-up rivers with a known answer; a check that the lagged features of the simple models stop at the issue day.
    • Spatial check: leave-one-basin-out training, and a model trained on the other complete-record basins scored on rivers it never saw.
    • A second, independent reviewer recomputes every headline number from the stored forecasts with its own code.
    NSENash–Sutcliffe efficiency, median over basins
    KGEKling–Gupta efficiency
    warnedshare of flood onsets warned (POD)
    falseshare of warnings that were false (FAR)
    CSIhits ÷ (hits + misses + false)

    A flood onset is a day above the level exceeded on 5% of the training days, forecast while the river was still at or below it. A warning is a forecast above that level.

    GOat least 70% of onsets warned and at most 30% of the warnings false
    CAUTIONnot GO, but a critical success index of at least 0.40
    NOT YETneither: the warnings are not to be used operationally

    A gain is clear only if every training with the extra input is above every training without it and above its control, and, for NSE, higher in most basins. Ranges come from the trainings and from test years and basins drawn again.
